🔑 1. What Are Zero Knowledge Proofs?
KEY IDEA: A zero-knowledge proof is a proof where the Prover convinces the Verifier that a statement is true WITHOUT revealing any information beyond the truth of the statement.
Example: Proving you know a password without revealing the password itself. The verifier becomes convinced you have it, but learns nothing about what the password actually is.

Inventors: Goldwasser, Micali, and Rackoff (1985) — "Knowledge Complexity of Interactive Proof Systems"

Key Readings:
• Textbook: Chapter 11 (p. 337-367)
• Quisquater "How to Explain ZKP to Your Children" (Ali Baba cave)
• Goldwasser et al. "Knowledge Complexity of Interactive Proof Systems"
• ZK-STARKs Medium posts (Parts 1-3)
• Zilch framework paper
• "Reflections on Trusting Trust" (Ken Thompson)
📜 2. Three Properties of ZKP
Remember These Three
Completeness • Soundness • Zero-Knowledge
Property Definition What It Prevents
Completeness If the statement is true, an honest Prover can convince an honest Verifier Prevents false negatives (honest provers rejected)
Soundness If the statement is false, no cheating Prover can convince the Verifier (except with negligible probability) Prevents false positives (liars accepted)
Zero-Knowledge The Verifier learns NOTHING beyond whether the statement is true Prevents information leakage
Threat Model: The threat model assumes potential cheating behavior from the Prover. The system must be sound even against a malicious Prover trying to convince the Verifier of a false statement.
🏴 3. The Ali Baba Cave Analogy (Quisquater)
The Setup: Peggy (Prover) wants to prove she knows the secret word to open a door inside a circular cave. Victor (Verifier) wants to be convinced without learning the secret.

How It Works:

  1. Victor waits outside while Peggy enters either path A or B
  2. Victor calls out which path Peggy should emerge from
  3. If Peggy knows the secret, she can always exit the correct side (opening the door if needed)
  4. If Peggy does NOT know the secret, she can only exit correctly 50% of the time (pure luck)
Ali Baba Cave Diagram
ENTRANCE | V +-----------+ | | Path A Path B | | +--- DOOR --+ (needs secret word) Round 1: Victor says "Come out Path A" - Peggy knows secret? She opens door if needed. PASS. - Peggy bluffing? 50% she's on wrong side. FAIL. After n rounds: Cheating probability = (1/2)^n 10 rounds: 1/1024 chance (~0.1%) 20 rounds: 1/1048576 chance (~0.0001%)
Why It's Zero-Knowledge: Victor never sees the door open, never learns the secret word. He only observes Peggy emerging from the correct path each time. Repeated trials make cheating probability negligible (1/2^n after n rounds).
🔄 4. Interactive vs Non-Interactive ZKP
Feature Interactive ZKP Non-Interactive ZKP
Communication Back-and-forth between Prover and Verifier Prover sends a single message (proof)
Example Ali Baba cave (multiple rounds) zk-SNARKs, zk-STARKs
Verifier Must be online and participate Anyone can verify the proof offline
Scalability Limited (requires interaction) Highly scalable (verify anytime)
Fiat-Shamir Heuristic: Transforms interactive ZKP into non-interactive by replacing the Verifier's random challenges with hash function output. The Prover "simulates" the Verifier using a cryptographic hash.
In non-interactive ZKP: Peggy proves she knows the secret information. She uses a one-way hash function to ensure Victor cannot learn any polynomial amount of information.
🔒 5. zk-SNARKs
Acronym
Zero-Knowledge Succinct Non-interactive Argument of Knowledge

Key Properties:

  • Zero-Knowledge: Verifier learns nothing beyond the statement's truth
  • Succinct: Proofs are short and quick to verify
  • Non-Interactive: Only one set of information is sent to verifier (single message)
  • Argument of Knowledge: Proves the Prover actually "knows" the information
Trusted Setup Vulnerability: zk-SNARKs require a "trusted setup" phase that creates initial parameters. If this setup is compromised, fake proofs become possible. This is their primary weakness.
Real-World Use: zk-SNARKs are used in Zcash for private transactions. Transactions are verified as valid without revealing sender, receiver, or amount.
💫 6. zk-STARKs
Acronym
Zero-Knowledge Scalable Transparent Argument of Knowledge
KEY ADVANTAGE: zk-STARKs require NO trusted setup (transparent). This eliminates the single biggest vulnerability of zk-SNARKs.

Key Properties:

  • Transparent: Uses public randomness instead of trusted setup
  • Quantum Computing Resistant: Uses hash functions, not elliptic curves
  • Scalable: Better performance at larger proof sizes
  • Larger proof sizes than SNARKs (trade-off for transparency)
Starkware is the company working on commercializing zk-STARKs.

According to the Medium post, average time savings in addressing issues: 6x-12x faster.

"Verifiable trust" — trust in computing systems achieved through ZK-STARKs. Rather than trusting a party, you trust the mathematical proof.
⚖️ 7. zk-SNARKs vs zk-STARKs Comparison
Feature zk-SNARKs zk-STARKs
Trusted Setup Required (vulnerability) NOT required (transparent)
Proof Size Smaller Larger
Verification Speed Faster Slightly slower
Quantum Resistance No (uses elliptic curves) Yes (uses hash functions)

Key Exam Point: The removal of the trusted setup phase is the primary advantage of STARKs over SNARKs. STARKs use public randomness; SNARKs use private setup.
🌐 8. Applications of Zero Knowledge Proofs

Real-World Applications:

  • Blockchain privacy: Zcash (private transactions), Ethereum zk-rollups (scaling)
  • Identity verification: Prove identity without revealing personal data
  • Age verification: Prove you are over 21 without showing your ID
  • Password authentication: Prove you know the password without transmitting it
Smart contract enabled blockchain: Provides immutable verification of proofs. Once a zero-knowledge proof is verified on-chain, it cannot be tampered with.
Privacy Enhancement: ZKP enhances privacy by reducing the risk of data breaches. If you never transmit the sensitive data, it cannot be intercepted or stolen.
🐝 9. Reflections on Trusting Trust (Ken Thompson, 1984)
KEY IDEA: You can't fully trust code you didn't write yourself — even the compiler could be compromised.

Context: Ken Thompson's 1984 Turing Award lecture, one of the most famous papers in computer science.

The Self-Replicating Trojan Horse:

  1. Modify the compiler to insert a backdoor into a specific program (e.g., login program)
  2. Modify the compiler to insert the modification into future versions of itself
  3. Remove all evidence from the compiler source code
  4. The backdoor persists even if the source code is completely clean
Self-Replicating Trojan Chain
Clean Source Code | V [Compromised Compiler] ---> Inserts backdoor into login | V [Recompiled Compiler] ---> Still compromised! | (backdoor propagates) V Source code looks clean, binary is infected Result: UNDETECTABLE by source code review
Main challenge of detecting Trojans in low-level programs: They are often well-hidden. You cannot detect them by reading the source code because the compromise lives in the compiled binary, not the source.
Thompson's View: Ken Thompson believes "more balanced media portrayal of computer crimes" is needed to bridge the cultural gap between hackers and society.
Lesson: Trust is fundamental and hard to establish in computing. This connects to ZKP because zero-knowledge proofs offer a way to establish trust mathematically, without needing to trust the other party.
🛡️ 10. ZKP in Privacy-Preserving Systems
Core Benefit: Zero-knowledge proofs can enhance privacy by reducing the risk of data breaches. If sensitive data is never transmitted or stored, it cannot be stolen.

Privacy Applications:

  • Prove compliance without revealing sensitive data (audits, regulations)
  • Enable verification without data exposure
  • Reduce attack surface by minimizing data in transit
Important Distinction: Minimum-disclosure proofs vs zero-knowledge proofs. Minimum-disclosure proofs reveal MORE information than zero-knowledge proofs. ZKP is the gold standard for privacy preservation.
Type Information Revealed Privacy Level
Full Disclosure Everything None
Minimum Disclosure More than necessary Partial
Zero Knowledge Nothing beyond truth of statement Maximum
🔧 11. Pinocchio — Verifiable Computation
Core Concept: Pinocchio was the first general-purpose system for efficiently verifying computations outsourced to the cloud, relying only on cryptographic assumptions.

How It Works:

  • Client creates a public evaluation key that describes the computation
  • Worker evaluates the computation and uses the evaluation key to produce a proof of correctness
  • Proof is only 288 bytes, regardless of computation size

Key Performance Metrics:

MetricDetail
Verification time5–7 orders of magnitude less than previous work (~10ms)
Proof effort reduction19–60x reduction for the worker
Proof size288 bytes (constant, regardless of computation)
Why It Matters: Pinocchio was the first system to demonstrate that verification can be cheaper than native execution for some applications.
  • Generalizes to zero-knowledge proofs at negligible additional cost
  • End-to-end toolchain: compiles a subset of C into verifiable computation programs
🔐 12. Self-Sovereign Identity & ZKP
Core Idea: Personal data (DOB, SSN) contributes to our identity. Self-sovereign identity means individuals own and manage their own data, shifting away from organizations owning it.

The Problem:

  • Facebook’s data mining and Equifax’s breach showed organizations cannot be trusted with our data
  • Traditional identity systems require sharing sensitive PII

The ZKP Solution:

  • zk-SNARKs enable proving identity without sharing sensitive information
  • Cryptographic proof cannot be linked to the data used to create it
  • With ZKP, Equifax-type leaks could become a thing of the past
Key Shift: From “organizations own our data” to “individuals control their own data.”

💰 14. Zcash — zk-SNARKs in Practice
First Widespread Application: Zcash was the first widespread application of zk-SNARKs. Shielded transactions are fully encrypted on the blockchain but still verifiable under consensus rules.

Trusted Setup Ceremony:

  • Requires an elaborate multi-party ceremony
  • Original ceremony: 6 participants, 5 revealed their identities
  • The 6th used pseudonym “John Dobbertin” — later revealed to be Edward Snowden
  • New ceremony: organized with 88 participants to address weaknesses

Zerocash Protocol:

  • Builds on Zerocoin, addresses its shortcomings
  • Proof sizes are small and fast to verify
  • All transaction amounts are hidden
Cons: Computationally intensive, complicated setup, incorrect implementation can lead to coin forgery.
⚛️ 15. Quantum Resistant vs Quantum Proof
Important Distinction from Lecture: These terms are NOT interchangeable.
TermAnalogyMeaning
Quantum ResistantWater RESISTANT watch (resists to a certain depth)Resists quantum attacks to a degree
Quantum ProofWATERPROOF watch (impervious)Completely immune to quantum attacks
  • Until the true capabilities of quantum computers are known, we can only claim resistance, not proof
  • zk-STARKs are currently quantum RESISTANT (use hash functions, not ECC)
  • zk-SNARKs rely on elliptic curves — vulnerable to quantum attacks
